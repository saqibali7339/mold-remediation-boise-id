Removal done in the right order

Mold Remediation in Boise, ID

Scrubbing visible spots doesn’t remove mold — it spreads spores to the next room. Real remediation seals the area, removes what can’t be cleaned, and proves the space is clear before walls go back up.

How it works

The remediation sequence, step by step

This order is laid out in the IICRC S520 standard and the EPA remediation guide.

01
Day 1

Assess the extent

Map visible growth, then check hidden cavities with moisture readings. The EPA treats areas over about 10 sq ft as a job for trained professionals.

02
Day 1

Fix the moisture first

A leak, a drain line or high crawlspace humidity — remediation without this step just resets the clock.

03
Day 1–2

Build containment

6-mil poly walls, a zip door, and negative air pressure through a HEPA air scrubber so spores move out of the house, not into it.

04
Day 2–3

Remove & clean

Porous materials like drywall, carpet pad and insulation are bagged and removed. Framing is HEPA-vacuumed, wire-brushed or sanded, then damp-wiped.

05
Day 3–4

Dry & verify

Materials are dried to normal readings. Many homeowners add independent post-remediation testing before rebuild.

06
After clearance

Rebuild

New insulation, drywall and finishes go in only once the space has passed its check.

Causes

Where remediation jobs usually start in the valley

Crawlspaces

Summer groundwater

When irrigation water is flowing, parts of the valley see the water table rise. Crawlspace joists absorb that humidity and grow mold on the north side first.

Attics

Bath fans venting into the attic

Exhaust ducts that dead-end in insulation instead of reaching the roof cap leave warm, wet air on cold winter sheathing.

Behind walls

Slow plumbing seeps

A pinhole leak in a supply line or a weeping dishwasher connection can feed growth for months before anything shows.

Prevention tips

Keep it from coming back

Hold indoor humidity under 50%The EPA’s target range is 30–50%. A $15 hygrometer tells you where you stand.

Run the bath fan 20 minutes after showersAnd confirm the duct actually exits the roof or a wall cap.

Check the crawlspace every JulyPeak irrigation season is when standing water and damp vapor barriers show up.

Dry any leak within 24–48 hoursThat’s the window the CDC and EPA give before growth begins.

FAQs

Remediation questions, answered plainly

Does Idaho require a license for mold remediation?

Idaho doesn’t have a state mold-specific contractor license the way some states do. That makes it more important to ask what standard a company follows (IICRC S520 is the industry reference) and whether techs hold IICRC certifications. Check the Idaho DOPL for general contractor registration.

Can I stay in the house during mold remediation?

For most contained jobs, yes. Containment plus negative air pressure keeps spores out of the living space. People with asthma, infants or immune conditions are often advised to stay elsewhere during the removal days.

Will bleach kill mold on drywall?

No. The EPA advises against relying on bleach: it doesn’t reach the roots inside porous material, and the water in it can feed regrowth. Moldy drywall gets removed, not sprayed.

How much does mold remediation cost in Boise?

It depends mostly on square footage, access (crawlspace and attic work costs more), and how much material has to be replaced. A small, contained bathroom job and a whole-crawlspace treatment can differ by thousands. A free inspection gives you a real number.

What is post-remediation verification?

An independent inspection — often with air samples — after cleanup but before rebuild, confirming spore levels inside match normal outdoor or untouched-room levels. See mold testing.

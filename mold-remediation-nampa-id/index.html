Why choose us

Local experts. Real results.

The IICRC S520 is the industry's reference for professional mold remediation. It lays out how to assess the extent of growth, set up containment, remove contaminated materials, and verify the work — step by step, with safety controls throughout.

Key principles include fixing the moisture source before remediation begins, using HEPA filtration to control airborne particles, and establishing clear criteria for when an area is done. The standard also addresses worker protection and how to handle contents within the work area.

You don't need to read the standard yourself. But knowing the work follows a published framework — rather than improvisation — is how you tell professional remediation from a spray-and-pray outfit.

Approaches

Three ways growth gets handled

Not every mold problem needs the same response. The right approach depends on how far it has spread and what it is growing on.

🧽

Clean in place

Small patches on tile, glass, or metal get scrubbed and dried. No demolition, no containment — just thorough cleaning.

🔨

Cut and replace

Moldy drywall and insulation come out in sealed bags. Framing gets HEPA-vacuumed and treated before new board goes up.

🛡️

Contained remediation

Larger areas get plastic containment, negative air, and a step-by-step removal plan following IICRC S520 principles.

Frequently asked questions

Your mold questions, answered.

How is remediation different from just cleaning the mold off?

Cleaning removes what's visible. Remediation removes the colony, the moisture feeding it, and the contaminated material — then verifies the area is dry.

Will remediation damage my walls or floors?

Contaminated drywall usually needs cutting out, but framing is typically cleaned and kept. The goal is to remove as little as possible while getting all of it.

How do you keep spores from spreading during the work?

The work area is sealed with plastic sheeting and kept under negative air pressure with a HEPA-filtered machine. That keeps airborne particles inside the zone.

Does the EPA recommend a specific approach?

The EPA's mold guidance emphasizes fixing the moisture problem first, then removing mold from hard surfaces and discarding porous materials that can't be cleaned.

What happens after the mold is removed?

The area is dried, cleaned with a HEPA vacuum, and checked with moisture readings. You'll get notes on keeping it dry going forward.

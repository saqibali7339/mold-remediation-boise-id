Local issues

Why Eagle homes get damp

May–Jun

Boise River high water

When upstream reservoirs release spring runoff, groundwater near the river rises. Crawlspaces in river-corridor subdivisions can take on seepage for weeks.

Yearround

More fixtures, more risk

Bigger homes mean more bathrooms, wet bars and upstairs laundry — more supply lines to fail above finished ceilings.

Winter

Vacant second homes

Seasonal homes left at low heat can freeze pipes that go unnoticed until the owner returns.

Permits for rebuild work: City of Eagle Building Department. Flood zone maps: FEMA Map Service Center.

FAQs

Eagle mold questions

Is my Eagle home in a flood zone?

Parts of Eagle near the Boise River channels are mapped in FEMA flood zones. You can look up your address on the FEMA Map Service Center; being in or near a zone affects both insurance and crawlspace moisture.

Why does my crawlspace get wet in June even though it’s not raining?

That timing lines up with river high water and irrigation deliveries, both of which raise shallow groundwater near the river. A sump pump and sealed vapor barrier are common fixes.

How can I protect a second home from frozen pipes?

Keep the thermostat at 55°F or higher, shut off the main when away for long stretches, and add a Wi-Fi leak or temperature sensor so you hear about problems before they spread.

Should I test for mold before buying a home in Eagle?

For homes with finished basements or large crawlspaces — especially near the river — an inspection during the buying period is a reasonable step. See mold testing.
